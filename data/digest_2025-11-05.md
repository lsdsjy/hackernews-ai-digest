### Staying opinionated as you grow

#### [Submission URL](https://hugo.writizzy.com/being-opinionated/57a0fa35-1afc-4824-8d42-3bce26e94ade) | 35 points | by [hlassiege](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=hlassiege) | [21 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45823000)

# The Power of Being Opinionated: How Strong Beliefs Build Better Products  

**"Build products with a backbone—or risk becoming everything to no one."**  

Hugo, founder of a 600+ person company, reflects on how scale often kills authenticity—in both product design and messaging. His key insight? **Growth demands tough choices**, not endless compromise.  

### Why Saying "No" is a Superpower  
- **Enshittification is real**: As products grow, they often become bloated by trying to please everyone. Examples? Look at software that’s morphed into a Frankenstein of features.  
- **Complexity ≠ Complicated**: Great products hide complexity, like Apple’s "it just works" philosophy. Bad ones dump it on users.  
- **Cut ruthlessly**: Killing features or ignoring niche demands keeps products sharp.  

### The Death (and Revival) of Authentic Messaging  
Early-stage companies have clarity: their messaging is raw, opinionated, and direct. But as marketing teams take over, the edge often fades into bland, "universally appealing" drivel.  

**Case in point**: Writizzy’s homepage is a manifesto—just a bold statement of purpose:  
> *"A blogging platform that doesn’t waste your time."*  

No fluff, no feature grids, no "10x ROI" buzzwords. Just attitude. Critics asked: *Why not say "saves time"?* Answer: **Because they’re not here to placate—they’re here to challenge**.  

### The Takeaway  
- **Hook fast**: Visitors decide in 10 seconds. A strong POV ("We’re *not* X") cuts through noise.  
- **Embrace opposition**: Frustration fuels innovation. Hugo’s team built their product as a rebellion against over-engineered, engagement-chasing platforms.  
- **Growth ≠ selling out**: Will Writizzy someday add flashy marketing blocks? Maybe. But for now, it stands as proof that **clarity beats consensus**.  

**Final thought**: In a world of middling corporate speak, being unapologetically opinionated isn’t just refreshing—it’s a strategic advantage.
