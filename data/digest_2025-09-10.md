### KDE launches its own distribution

#### [Submission URL](https://lwn.net/SubscriberLink/1037166/caa6979c16a99c9e/) | 627 points | by [Bogdanp](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=Bogdanp) | [437 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45204393)

**KDE Launches Its Own Immutable Linux Distro: A Modern Take on the Desktop OS**  

The KDE Project has unveiled **KDE Linux**, a new **immutable distribution** built from scratch to showcase the best of KDE’s ecosystem. Announced at **Akademy 2025**, this distro aims to provide a polished, cutting-edge experience for home users, businesses, and OEMs—though it’s still in **alpha** with rough edges.  

### **Why Another Distro?**  
KDE contributor **Nate Graham** addressed the inevitable question—*why create yet another Linux distribution?*—by pointing out that many major desktop environments, like GNOME (with its own OS efforts) and ElementaryOS, have their own dedicated OS. He argued that KDE, as a major software producer, deserves a platform to showcase its vision unimpeded by legacy constraints.  

This isn’t KDE’s first foray into distribution-building—**KDE neon** has been around for years, but its **Ubuntu LTS base** has limitations, requiring extensive patching and ultimately detracting from stability. KDE Linux breaks free from those constraints with a **fresh, modern approach**.  

### **Tech Choices & Philosophy**  
KDE Linux makes some **bold architectural decisions**:  
- **Immutable design** with **Btrfs** (read/write `/`) and **EROFS** (read-only `/usr`).  
- **Wayland-only**, with no X.org support. Nvidia users (especially on older GPUs) will need manual tweaks.  
- **UEFI-only**, leaving behind BIOS legacy systems.  
- Updates are **atomic**, leveraging `systemd-sysupdate` (though delta updates aren’t supported yet).  
- **No traditional package management**—users get apps via **Flatpak** (integrated with Discover) or **Snap** (CLI-only for now).  
- Includes **Distrobox**, letting users run traditional packages in containers.  

### **Early Challenges**  
The alpha isn’t without hiccups. Testing **Distrobox** revealed missing UID/GID mappings, a known issue requiring manual fixes. And with **whole-system updates (~4.8GB each)** consuming significant disk space, KDE Linux currently expects users to allocate **30GB just for version rollbacks**—an area where efficiency improvements are needed.  

### **What’s Next?**  
KDE Linux is positioned as a **visionary** rather than a universal replacement. Graham acknowledges that users who dislike its choices can stick with other distros—but for those who want a **clean, modern KDE-first experience**, this could be an exciting option down the road.

### Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference

#### [Submission URL](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/) | 296 points | by [jxmorris12](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=jxmorris12) | [124 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45200925)

# **The Hidden Culprit Behind Inconsistent LLM Outputs—And How to Fix It**  

You’ve probably noticed that asking the same question to ChatGPT multiple times yields different answers—even when you set the temperature to zero, which should make responses completely deterministic. This isn’t just a quirk of OpenAI’s API; even running open-source LLMs locally with tools like **vLLM** or **SGLang** doesn’t guarantee identical outputs.  

### 🔍 **What’s Going On? The Floating-Point Conundrum**  

The usual explanation is **“concurrency + floating-point errors”**—that GPUs perform parallel computations in an unpredictable order, and floating-point arithmetic isn’t associative, meaning $(a + b) + c \neq a + (b + c)$. Tiny rounding differences could theoretically snowball into different outputs.  

But here’s the surprising part: If you run the exact same matrix multiplication repeatedly on a GPU, the results **are** identical. So why do LLMs behave differently?  

### 🎭 **The Real Problem Lies Elsewhere**  

The truth is more nuanced. While GPU kernels themselves are deterministic, **the way LLMs process sequences introduces unpredictability**—particularly in **attention mechanisms and softmax computations**.  

- **Attention Scores:** When computing self-attention, the model evaluates relationships between tokens in parallel.  
- **Softmax Nondeterminism:** Even slight numerical differences in intermediate steps (due to floating-point rounding) can shift which token is selected in sampling.  

Normally, if temperature is zero (greedy decoding), you’d expect consistency—but in practice, softmax isn’t always bitwise identical due to GPU optimizations.  

### 💡 **How to Defeat Nondeterminism**  

1. **Disable Tensor Cores (for CUDA)** – Tensor cores speed up matrix ops but may introduce slight numerical variation.  
2. **Enforce Synchronization** – Prevent race conditions by forcing deterministic CUDA execution.  
3. **Fix the Random Seed** – Ensure that *all* random elements (e.g., dropout) are controlled.  
4. **Use Float32 Instead of BFloat16** – Higher precision reduces floating-point artifacts.  

### 🚀 **Reality Check: Is True Determinism Possible?**  

Even with all these fixes, **hardware-level unpredictability** (like thermal throttling affecting compute order) can still cause tiny deviations. But for most practical purposes, these tweaks can get you **99.9% reproducibility**—good enough for research and debugging.  

### **Final Thought**  

Floating-point math was never designed to be perfectly associative—it was built for **speed**, not precision. Overcoming nondeterminism means balancing efficiency and control. The battle isn’t over yet, but at least we now know where to aim.

### **Intel's Next-Gen IPU: The Mount Morgan Unveiled – More Power, More Efficiency, More Offloads**  

At **Hot Chips 2025**, Intel pulled back the curtain on its latest **Infrastructure Processing Unit (IPU)**—**Mount Morgan (E2200)**—with major upgrades over its predecessor, **Mount Evans**. Designed to tackle cloud infrastructure workloads, the new IPU brings more **Arm cores, better accelerators, and double the Ethernet throughput**, making it a powerhouse for next-gen data centers.  

### **Why IPUs Matter**  
Cloud providers juggle two key challenges:  
1. **Maximizing compute resources**—Every CPU core dedicated to managing infrastructure (like VM provisioning, networking, and security) is a core that *can’t* be rented to customers.  
2. **Security & isolation**—Running infrastructure services on separate silicon (like an IPU) prevents provider-side software from interfering with customer workloads.  

Enter **Mount Morgan**, Intel’s latest IPU—a **supercharged smart NIC** with its own **Arm-based CPU, accelerators, and flexible deployment modes**.  

---  
### **Mount Morgan’s Upgrades at a Glance**  

| Feature | Mount Evans | Mount Morgan |  
|---------|-------------|--------------|  
| **CPU Cores** | 16x Arm Neoverse N1 | **24x Arm Neoverse N2** (more IPC, bigger out-of-order window) |  
| **Memory** | 48GB LPDDR4X-4267 (triple-channel) | **64GB LPDDR5-6400 (quad-channel)** (~51.2GB/s bandwidth) |  
| **System Cache** | 32MB | **32MB (no change, but likely optimal for cost/hit rate)** |  
| **Ethernet** | 200Gbps | **400Gbps (double bandwidth!)** |  
| **Crypto/Compression (LCE)** | Symmetric-only (QAT-style) | **Now supports asymmetric (RSA, ECDHE for TLS!)** + DMA engine for smarter data flows |  
| **Network Processing** | P4-based FXP (Flexible eXecution Pipeline) | **Enhanced FXP—faster, multi-pass packet parsing & routing at line rate** |  

---  
### **Key Highlights**  

#### **1. Compute: 24 Neoverse N2 Cores – More Muscle for Infrastructure Workloads**  
Mount Morgan packs **24x Arm Neoverse N2 cores**, a big step up from Mount Evans’ 16x N1 cores. The **N2 architecture** (featured in AWS’s Graviton3) is more efficient, with deeper out-of-order execution, better branch prediction, and **higher IPC**.  

Intel doesn’t specify clocks, but if they match the previous-gen IPU (~2.5 GHz), this could be **on par with some real server workloads**—not just infrastructure tasks.  

#### **2. Networking: 400G Ethernet + Smarter Packet Processing**  
Cloud providers need **high bandwidth + low overhead**, and Mount Morgan now supports **400Gbps Ethernet (2x Mount Evans’ 200Gbps)**.  

The **FXP (Flexible eXecution Pipeline)**—Intel’s **P4-programmable packet engine**—now handles **multi-pass processing per packet**. For example:  
- **First pass:** Decapsulate outer layers (think VXLAN, NVMe-over-Fabrics).  
- **Second pass:** Apply security checks (ACLs) or process inner payloads.  

This is all done at **line rate (!)**, meaning **zero performance hits** while doing deep packet inspection.  

#### **3. Crypto Finally Gets Asymmetric Support (TLS Handshake Offload!)**  
A major limitation of Mount Evans was that its **Lookaside Crypto Engine (LCE)** could only accelerate **symmetric crypto** (AES, SHA).  

**Mount Morgan fixes this by adding RSA & ECDSA/ECDHE acceleration**—critical for **TLS handshakes**, which otherwise chew up server CPU cycles.  

The LCE also now has a **programmable DMA engine**, letting cloud providers **chain compute & data movement** (e.g., storing encrypted, compressed data to remote storage **without CPU involvement**).  

---  
### **Why This Matters for the Cloud**  
- **More CPU for customers** – Offloading infrastructure tasks to Mount Morgan means **more cores available for revenue-generating workloads**.  
- **Faster networking & storage** – 400G Ethernet + smarter accelerators mean **lower latency and higher throughput** for things like NVMe-over-Fabrics storage.  
- **Security & resilience** – Keeping provider-side software *off* customer CPUs means **no accidental resource contention or side-channel leaks**.  

---  
### **Final Thought: IPUs Are Now Mini Servers**  
Mount Morgan blurs the line between **NIC, accelerator, and standalone server**. With **24 Arm cores, 64GB RAM, and PCIe attachability**, it could feasibly run **edge workloads** independently.  

Is this the future? Intel seems to think so—**general-purpose CPUs + specialized offloads**, all managed securely from the IPU.

### **How Kerberoasting Works (The Horror Show)**  

1. **Active Directory & Kerberos:** AD uses the ancient **Kerberos protocol** (born in **1989**) to authenticate users trying to access network services (like file servers).  
2. **The Ticket System:** When you request access to a service, AD gives you an encrypted **"ticket"** locked with the service account’s password.  
3. **The Fatal Mistake:** If an admin accidentally sets up a service with a **human-generated password** instead of a strong cryptographic key, attackers can steal these tickets.  
4. **Offline Cracking:** Hackers grab the encrypted ticket, take it offline, and **brute-force the password** using dictionary attacks (thanks, weak keys!).  
5. **Total Takeover:** Once cracked, they gain full control of the service—**leading to ransomware, data theft, and chaos**.  

### **Why Is This Still a Problem?**  

- **Legacy Code Lock-In:** AD’s Kerberos implementation still supports defunct ciphers like **RC4** (a cryptographer’s nightmare).  
- **Human Error in IT:** Some admins still use **weak passwords** for service accounts, making cracking trivial.  
- **Microsoft’s Slow Fixes:** Despite years of warnings, **Enterprise IT moves at glacial speed**.  

### **Real-World Damage**

### **Can We Fix It?**  

- **Enforce Strong Passwords:** Microsoft *could* prevent admins from using weak keys. (Spoiler: They don’t always.)  
- **Rotate Service Keys Automatically:** Reduce exposure to brute-force attacks.  
- **Upgrade AD Cryptography:** Dump outdated algorithms (looking at you, RC4).

# **A Love Letter to CSV: The Unsung Hero of Data Formats**

CSV (Comma-Separated Values) might seem like the most unglamorous format in data science—no nested structures, no schemas, just plain text with commas. And yet, in a world of JSON, XML, and complex binary formats, the humble CSV persists as the *lingua franca* of data exchange.

## **Why CSV?**  
- **Simplicity**: A CSV is just a text file—no parsing gymnastics required.  
- **Universality**: Almost every tool, from Excel to Python's `pandas`, can read it.  
- **Human-Readable**: Unlike cryptic binary formats, you can open it in Notepad and *understand it*.  

## **But… It’s Not Perfect**  
- **Escaping chaos**: Commas in values? Quotes? Line breaks? CSV handles them… *kinda*.  
- **No enforced schema**: One missing column and your script might silently break.  
- **Encoding woes**: Is this UTF-8? Latin-1? Who knows!  

## **The Verdict**  
CSV is like the duct tape of data—messy at times, but indispensable when you’re in a pinch. It doesn’t pretend to be smart, and that’s exactly why we love it.

### **Final Takeaway**
