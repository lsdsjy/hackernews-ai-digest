### Bear is now source-available

#### [Submission URL](https://herman.bearblog.dev/license/) | 495 points | by [neoromantique](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=neoromantique) | [439 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45092490)

# **Bear Switches from Open Source to Source-Available Amid Forking Concerns**  

### **A Painful Decision to Protect Years of Work**  

Bear, the privacy-focused note-taking app, is moving away from its MIT open-source license to a **source-available** model. Founder Shiny Frog made the tough call after repeated instances of forks turning into **direct competitors**, undermining the project's sustainability.  

### **Why the Change?**  
- **Open-source hurt more than it helped**: Despite the initial intent (transparency, auditability), forks repurposed Bear’s code into competing services with minimal effort.  
- **"It hurts"**: The emotional toll of seeing years of work copied in hours was too much.  
- **AI-powered competition exacerbates the issue**: With tools like AI making forking and deployment trivial, protecting the project's uniqueness becomes harder.  

### **New License: Elastic License**  
- **Similar to MIT… but with restrictions**: The new license prevents using Bear’s code to run **managed/hosted services** (i.e., no more Bear clones-as-a-service).  
- **Follows industry trends**: Elastic (Elasticsearch), MongoDB, and others have taken similar steps to prevent free-riding competition.  

### **Looking Ahead**  
The priority remains **long-term sustainability**—protecting Bear’s core community while keeping the code auditable. It’s a bittersweet shift, but one more developers are facing in the AI era.

### Adaptive LLM routing under budget constraints

#### [Submission URL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21141) | 198 points | by [tdchaitanya](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=tdchaitanya) | [77 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45094421)

## AI-Powered LLM Routing: The Future of Smart, Budget-Conscious AI Assistance  

As large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4, Claude, and Llama continue to grow in capability *and* cost, efficiently routing user queries to the right model is becoming crucial. A new research paper, *"Adaptive LLM Routing under Budget Constraints"* (Panda et al., EMNLP 2025), proposes an innovative approach to dynamically match queries with the best-suited LLM while staying within budget.  

### **The Problem: One-Size-Fits-All Doesn’t Cut It**  
LLMs differ in performance and pricing—some excel at coding, others at creative writing, and some are lightweight but cost-effective. Supervised learning approaches assume we already know which model is best for every query, but in reality:  
- **Optimal routing isn’t predefined**—new queries emerge constantly.  
- **Feedback is sparse**—we usually only see the output of the chosen model, not alternatives.  
- **Budget constraints matter**—users and companies can’t afford to always use the most expensive model.  

### **The Solution: A Bandit-Based Approach**  
The researchers frame LLM routing as a **contextual bandit problem**, a reinforcement learning paradigm where an algorithm learns which actions (routing choices) yield the best rewards (quality responses) based on partial feedback. Their method, **PILOT (Preference-prior Informed LinUCB for adaptive rouTing)**, combines:  
- **Offline knowledge**: Pre-trained embeddings of queries and LLMs, initialized using human preference data.  
- **Online learning**: Continuously refines routing decisions based on real user feedback.  
- **Budget awareness**: Treats model selection as a **multi-choice knapsack problem**, optimizing for *both* performance and cost.  

### **Why This Matters**  
- **Cost savings**: Avoids unnecessary calls to expensive LLMs when cheaper ones suffice.  
- **Adaptability**: Learns in real-time as queries and model capabilities evolve.  
- **Scalability**: Works without needing exhaustive testing across all models for every query.  

### **What’s Next?**  
This approach could power next-gen **AI orchestration layers**, helping businesses and developers dynamically balance cost and quality in AI workflows. The preprint is on [arXiv](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.21141), with plans for presentation at **EMNLP 2025**—mark your calendars for smarter, thriftier AI!

### The first inkjet printer was a medical device

#### [Submission URL](https://spectrum.ieee.org/rune-elmqvist) | 29 points | by [benbreen](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=benbreen) | [3 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45089072)

### **The Unexpected Origin of the Inkjet Printer: A Life-Saving Medical Innovation**

Most people associate inkjet printers with office documents or home photo printing, but the very first inkjet printer was invented for an entirely different purpose—*saving lives*.  

#### **Meet the Inventor: Rune Elmqvist, Doctor-Turned-Engineer**  
Swedish inventor **Rune Elmqvist** was initially trained as a physician but chose a different path—engineering medical devices. His creations revolutionized both healthcare and technology.  

#### **The Mingograph (1949): The First Inkjet Printer**  
Before Elmqvist, medical devices like electrocardiogram (ECG) machines used mechanical styli to record heart signals. But friction between the stylus and paper limited accuracy. Elmqvist's breakthrough? **A nozzle spraying electrically controlled ink droplets onto paper**—eliminating friction and enabling precise real-time readings of heart and brain activity.  

#### **A Legacy Beyond Printing: The First Implantable Pacemaker**  
Eight years after inventing the Mingograph, Elmqvist teamed up with surgeon **Åke Senning** to create another medical marvel—the **first fully implantable pacemaker** in 1958. This device has since saved millions of lives.  

#### **Why This Matters**  
- **Medical Origins of Everyday Tech**: Inkjet printing, now ubiquitous, was born from a need to improve medical diagnostics.  
- **Dual Impact**: Elmqvist’s contributions span both medical innovation and computing history.  

Next time you print a document or hear about a pacemaker saving a life, remember—**it all traces back to one man’s brilliant pivot from medicine to engineering.**

### CocoaPods trunk read-only plan

#### [Submission URL](https://blog.cocoapods.org/CocoaPods-Specs-Repo/) | 243 points | by [matharmin](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=matharmin) | [111 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45091493)

**CocoaPods Trunk Going Read-Only in 19 Months: What You Need to Know**  

CocoaPods, the popular dependency manager for Swift and Objective-C projects, is undergoing a major change. The **CocoaPods trunk (specs repo) will transition to read-only by December 2026**, meaning no new Pods or updates will be accepted after that date. Here’s what you need to know:  

🔹 **Why?**  
- **Security & Maintenance Overhaul:** After years of dealing with security exploits (including abuse of the `prepare_command` field), the team is simplifying infrastructure by making trunk read-only.  
- **Existing Builds Unaffected:** All current Pods will remain available, hosted via GitHub and jsDelivr.  

🔹 **Timeline**  
- **May 2025:** New Pods using `prepare_command` will be blocked (existing ones grandfathered in).  
- **Late 2025 & Late 2026:** Mass emails to Podspec contributors warning of the change.  
- **November 2026:** A one-week "test run" of read-only mode.  
- **December 2, 2026:** Permanent switch to read-only (timed post-Thanksgiving for minimal disruption).  

🔹 **Who’s Impacted?**  
- Projects relying on **new/updated Pods from trunk** after Dec 2026 must migrate to alternative solutions (e.g., private specs repos, vendored dependencies, or SPM).  
- Self-hosted or vendored dependencies remain unchanged.  

🔹 **Need Help?**  
Reach out to the team:  
- Email: [support@cocoapods.org](mailto:support@cocoapods.org)  
- Orta (lead): [orta@cocoapods.org](mailto:orta@cocoapods.org) / [Bluesky (@orta.io)](https://bsky.app/profile/orta.io)  

This is a *long-term plan*—developers have **19 months** to adapt. Stay tuned for reminders, and share feedback if the timeline needs adjustment.
