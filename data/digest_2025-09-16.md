### **Making the Framework Desktop Whisper-Quiet: Noctua’s DIY Cooling Tweaks**  

If you’ve been enjoying the power of the **Framework Desktop**—AMD’s Ryzen AI Max APU-packed mini-PC—but wish it ran even **quieter**, Noctua has a solution—**and it involves a 3D printer!**  

#### **The Framework Desktop: Powerful but Potentially Noisy**  
The compact **Framework Desktop** is impressive—it packs a punch with AMD’s Ryzen AI Max APU, delivering laptop-grade power in a mini-PC form factor. But as Noctua discovered, the default cooling setup leaves room for **acoustic optimization**.  

#### **Noctua’s First Fix: A Fan & Duct Overhaul**  
Noctua initially improved cooling by integrating:  
- An **NF-A12x25 fan** (the legendary quiet performer).  
- A **custom fan duct** to optimize airflow.  

This reduced noise while keeping temps safe. **But they didn’t stop there.**  

#### **Making It Even Quieter: Custom Fan Grilles & More**  
To push noise levels further down, Noctua:  
- **Re-engineered their signature fan grill** (originally from the **Seasonic Prime TX-1600 Noctua Edition PSU**) to meet **safety standards** (vent openings <5mm).  
- Designed a **new funnel-shaped duct** to maximize airflow through the grille’s openings.  

**Results?** Up to **7 dB(A) quieter at lower speeds** and **5 dB(A) at higher speeds**—**without sacrificing cooling**.  

#### **Free 3D-Printable Upgrades**  
Noctua isn’t selling these parts—they’re releasing the **CAD files for free**! You can print:  
- **Custom side panel with Noctua-style grill** ([Download Here](https://printables.com))  
- **Optimized fan duct** ([Download Here](https://printables.com))  

With modern 3D printing, you can achieve a **clean, professional-looking mod** that noticeably **improves acoustics**.  

#### **Other Tweaks Tested (But Not All Worth It)**
- Adding an **NF-A8 exhaust fan**: Lowered temps **slightly** but **added extra noise**—not ideal.  
- Upgrading to the **NF-A12x25 G2**: Quieter by **1-1.5 dB(A)**, but **loses the high-speed thermal headroom** of the stock fan (2400 RPM vs. 1800 RPM).  

**Final Verdict?**  
- **Best upgrade?** The **custom side panel + duct** (if you can 3D print it).  
- **Biggest noise savings?** **Up to 7 dB(A)** at lower speeds.  
- **Other tweaks?** Only worth it if you’re okay with **sacrificing some performance for silence**.

### The "most hated" CSS feature: cos() and sin()

### **CSS Trigonometry: Why Are `sin()` and `cos()` So Hated? (And How to Actually Use Them)**  

The *"Most Hated CSS Feature"* award goes to… **trigonometric functions**! According to the *State of CSS 2025* survey, **9.1% of developers despise them**—but is the hate justified?  

In this post (the first in a series), we’ll dive into how `sin()` and `cos()` work, why they’re actually useful, and how you can leverage them to create stunning layouts—**without needing a math degree**.  

---  

### **What Even Are `sin()` and `cos()`?**  
If high school trig left you scarred, don’t worry—this isn’t *that* hard. Let’s keep it simple:  

- **The unit circle** is a circle with radius `1` plotted on a graph.  
- Any point on that circle can be located using **Cartesian coordinates (X, Y).**  
- `cos(angle)` gives the **X-coordinate**.  
- `sin(angle)` gives the **Y-coordinate**.  

That’s it. **Slide this demo** to see it in action:  

🔹 [**Interactive demo showing the connection between angles and `sin()`/`cos()`**]

### **Cool, But… Why Should I Care?**  
Because **circular layouts are awesome**, and CSS trigonometry makes them *effortless*.  

#### **Example: A Circular Menu**  
Imagine you have a set of elements (`<li>`) that you want to evenly space around a circle. Without trigonometry? Painful. **With `sin()` and `cos()`?** A breeze:  

```html
<ul style="--total: 9">
  <li style="--i: 0">0</li>
  <li style="--i: 1">1</li>
  <li style="--i: 2">2</li>
  <!-- ...up to 8 -->
</ul>
```

**The magic CSS:**  
```css
li {
  --angle: calc(360deg / var(--total) * var(--i)); 
  --radius: 10rem;
  --x: calc(cos(var(--angle)) * var(--radius));
  --y: calc(sin(var(--angle)) * var(--radius));
  transform: translate(var(--x), var(--y));
}
```

**Result:** A **perfectly spaced circular menu** where each item orbits around the center. And the best part? No JavaScript! 🎉
