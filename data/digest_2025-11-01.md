### **Hacker News Digest: Meet Visopsys – A Small, Fast, and Open-Source OS With a 26-Year Legacy**  

**Today’s Highlight:** Ever heard of an OS that’s been in development since **1997**, isn’t a clone of Windows/Linux, and still fits on a **floppy disk**? **Visopsys** (Visual Operating System) is exactly that—an independent, lightweight, and open-source OS for PC-compatible hardware.  

#### **Why Visopsys Stands Out**  
✔ **Tiny but Powerful** – Designed for efficiency with a **small footprint**, yet supports **pre-emptive multitasking** and **virtual memory**.  
✔ **Graphical & Functional** – Has its own **GUI** (no need for terminal-only agony).  
✔ **Not Just Another Clone** – Unlike ReactOS (Windows-like) or Linux distros, Visopsys is **built from scratch** with original design choices.  
✔ **Runs From Anything** – Bootable via **USB, CD/DVD, or even a floppy disk** (yes, floppies still matter!).  

#### **Latest Updates**  
The project is **actively maintained**, with the most recent release (**v0.92**) dropping in September 2023. Previous versions introduced stability fixes, hardware support, and performance tweaks, spanning over **two decades of development**.  

**Curious?** Grab the latest version [here](https://visopsys.org) or fire it up in a VM (it might just be the retro-futuristic OS you didn’t know you needed).

# arXiv Tightens Rules for Review and Position Papers in Computer Science  

### The Problem: AI-Generated Paper Flood  
arXiv’s computer science (CS) category is cracking down on low-quality **review articles** and **position papers** after a surge in submissions—many of which are **AI-generated bibliographies** lacking real scholarly insight. With **hundreds of these papers now flooding in monthly**, moderators are overwhelmed, delaying the processing of legitimate research.  

### The New Policy (Kinda)  
While **arXiv has never officially listed reviews or position papers as accepted content types**, moderators once allowed high-quality submissions from respected sources (like *Annual Reviews* or *Proceedings of the IEEE*). Now, **authors must prove their work has passed peer review** at a journal or conference *(not workshops!)* before arXiv will consider it.  

### Why the Change?  
- **Reduce noise**: Too many AI-written "surveys" drown out meaningful research.  
- **Save moderation time**: Volunteers can focus on arXiv’s core mission—sharing new research quickly.  
- **Leverage existing quality control**: Let journals and conferences vet reviews/position papers first.  

### What Counts as "Peer-Reviewed"?  
- A **journal acceptance** or **conference paper** with rigorous peer review.  
- Workshop reviews? **Not good enough.**  

### Exceptions?  
Research papers analyzing **science & society** (e.g., in `cs.CY` or `physics.soc-ph`) are **still welcome** without prior peer review.  

### TL;DR  
arXiv’s CS section is tired of sifting through AI slop. Now, if you want to post a review or position paper, **get it peer-reviewed elsewhere first**—or stick to original research.  

[Read the full arXiv announcement here.](https://info.arxiv.org/about/review-survey-policy-CS.html)
