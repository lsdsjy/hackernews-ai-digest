# Nobel Prize in Physics 2025 Awarded for Demonstrating Quantum Effects at Macroscopic Scale  

The 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics has been awarded to **John Clarke, Michel H. Devoret, and John M. Martinis** for their groundbreaking experiments that showed **quantum mechanics governing large, human-scale systems**—proving that the bizarre properties of particles can manifest in objects we can hold in our hands.  

## Key Discoveries:  
- **Macroscopic Quantum Tunneling**: The team demonstrated that a superconducting electrical circuit could suddenly shift from zero voltage to having voltage, as if it "tunneled" through an energy barrier (**like a ball teleporting through a wall**).  
- **Quantized Energy Absorption/Emissions**: Their system absorbed and released energy in precise "packets," confirming quantum behavior on a much larger scale than ever before.  

### Why It Matters:  
Quantum mechanics typically describes the behavior of **individual particles**, such as electrons tunneling through barriers at microscopic scales. Clarke, Devoret, and Martinis proved that even systems **millions of times larger**—built from countless particles working in sync—can exhibit the same strange phenomena.  

### The Experiment That Changed Everything:  
In the **mid-1980s**, the laureates designed a circuit with **two superconductors separated by an insulating layer**. Despite the insulator blocking current flow, the system **spontaneously generated voltage**, showcasing macroscopic quantum tunneling.  

### A Century of Quantum Mysteries:  
- **1928**: George Gamow first described quantum tunneling in atomic nuclei (explaining radioactive decay).  
- **2025**: This Nobel recognizes the extension of tunneling to **engineered, visible-sized systems**—opening doors to **quantum computing** and advanced materials.

### Eliminating contrails from flying could be cheap

#### [Submission URL](https://www.sustainabilitybynumbers.com/p/eliminating-contrails) | 142 points | by [K2L8M11N2](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=K2L8M11N2) | [178 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45507398)

### **A Surprisingly Simple Way to Cut Aviation’s Climate Impact in Half—And It’s Shockingly Cheap**  

A new report suggests that **eliminating airplane contrails**—those wispy white trails left in the sky—could **slash aviation’s contribution to global warming by nearly half**, and it could be done **for a fraction of the cost** of alternative solutions like sustainable aviation fuels.  

### **What Are Contrails, and Why Do They Matter?**  
Contrails form when **water vapor and soot** from jet engines freeze into ice crystals at high altitudes. These artificial clouds can **trap heat** in the Earth’s atmosphere, contributing to global warming. While they also reflect some sunlight (cooling the planet slightly), their **net effect is warming**—especially at night or in winter when there’s less sunlight to offset the heat-trapping effect.  

### **Contrails vs. CO₂: A Tale of Two Impacts**  
- **Contrails:** A **short but intense warming effect**—if we stopped them today, the warming would disappear within days.  
- **CO₂ emissions:** A **long-term problem**, with heat-trapping effects lasting for *centuries*.  

Right now, the **cumulative CO₂ emissions from aviation** and the **immediate warming from contrails** contribute roughly equal amounts to aviation’s total warming effect. That means **eliminating contrails could cut aviation’s climate impact in half**—*almost overnight*.  

### **The Cheap & Easy Fix? Just Reroute Planes Slightly**  
Startups like **Orca Sciences** (behind Contrails.org) are working on **real-time forecasting models** to predict where contrails form and **adjust flight paths to avoid them**. Since only **a small fraction of flights** create the majority of problematic contrails, the solution could be **as simple as optimizing altitude or route by a few thousand feet**.  

**The cost?** Estimates suggest it could be **less than $1 per passenger**—far cheaper than sustainable aviation fuels or carbon offsets.  

### **Not a Replacement for Cutting CO₂, But a Big Boost**  
While contrail reduction won’t solve aviation’s long-term CO₂ problem, it’s a **fast, low-cost win** that buys us time to develop cleaner fuels and tech. And since **airlines currently don’t measure or report contrail impacts**, even *acknowledging* this issue is a step forward.  

**Bottom Line:** Fixing contrails is a **no-brainer climate hack**—so why aren’t we doing it already?
