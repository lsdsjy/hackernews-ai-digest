### How did I get here?

#### [Submission URL](https://how-did-i-get-here.net/) | 255 points | by [zachlatta](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=zachlatta) | [51 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45850382)

**The Internet’s Secret Paths: How Traceroute Reveals Your Digital Journey**  

Ever wondered how data zips across the Internet to reach your screen? A clever tool called `ktr` (v0.6.1) is here to show you—by tracing the hidden path your connection takes.  

### **How It Works: A Reverse Traceroute Hack**  
When you visit [how-did-i-get-here.net](http://how-did-i-get-here.net), the site doesn’t just serve you static HTML—it *actively traces* the journey your request took. But here’s a twist: instead of tracking the path from *your device to the server*, it runs a **reverse traceroute**—from the server back to you—and flips the results.  

**Why?** Because the Internet’s routers don’t always take symmetrical paths. Your outbound route might differ from the return trip, but the approximation is close enough to reveal key network hops.  

### **ICMP Time-to-Live (TTL) Magic**  
`ktr` exploits a core Internet protocol, **ICMP**, which handles diagnostic messages. Every packet has a **TTL (Time to Live) counter**, acting like a hop limit:  
- Each router decrements the TTL before forwarding the packet.  
- If TTL hits *zero*, the router sends back an error with its IP.  
By sending packets with increasing TTLs (1, 2, 3…), `ktr` builds a hop-by-hop map of your connection.  

### **No JavaScript? No Problem.**  
This site *intentionally* avoids JavaScript. Instead, it streams HTML updates in real-time, hiding previous traceroute lines with **CSS hacks** as new data arrives. The result? A page that feels dynamic—even though it’s just a slow, cleverly structured document.  

### **The Internet is a Corporate Maze**  
Each hop in your traceroute belongs to an **Autonomous System (AS)**—a network controlled by entities like ISPs or cloud providers (e.g., Hetzner Online’s AS24940). These networks interconnect via **peering deals**, forming the Internet’s backbone. Want your own AS? Good luck—you’ll need deep pockets and multi-region infrastructure.  

**Fun Fact:** The Internet isn’t *really* decentralized—it’s a patchwork of corporate fiefdoms, governed by ASNs and bureaucratic RIRs (Regional Internet Registries).
