### Redis is fast – I'll cache in Postgres

#### [Submission URL](https://dizzy.zone/2025/09/24/Redis-is-fast-Ill-cache-in-Postgres/) | 292 points | by [redbell](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=redbell) | [250 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45380699)

# PostgreSQL vs Redis for Caching: A Performance Showdown  

The debate over using **PostgreSQL as a universal database**—even for caching—has gained traction. But how does it *actually* compare to **Redis**, the reigning king of caching? Let’s find out!  

### **The Experiment**  
A developer set up a **Kubernetes cluster** to benchmark **PostgreSQL (17.6)** vs **Redis (8.2)** as a cache layer for an HTTP server. Both were limited to **2 CPUs and 8GiB of RAM**, while a third node ran load tests using **k6**.  

### **The Setup**  
- A simple **HTTP server** exposed `/get` and `/set` endpoints for cache operations.  
- **Redis implementation**: Used `go-redis` with default settings.  
- **PostgreSQL implementation**: Used `pgx` with an **unlogged table** (to optimize for speed) for key-value storage.  
- **Workloads tested**:  
  - **GET-heavy** (80% cache hits)  
  - **SET-heavy** (10% updates)  
  - **Mixed workload** (80% reads, 20% writes)  

### **The Results**  

#### **1. GET Performance (Reads)**
✅ **Redis**:  
- **1,280 requests/sec**  
- **~3.8GB RAM usage**  
- **Bottleneck**: HTTP server CPU (not Redis itself)  

🐘 **PostgreSQL**:  
- **Slower, at CPU limit**  
- **~5GB RAM usage**  
- **Latency was higher** than Redis  

#### **2. SET Performance (Writes)**
✅ **Redis** again **outperformed** PostgreSQL:  
- Handled writes **faster**, still bottlenecked by the HTTP server.  
- RAM usage grew to **~4.3GB**.  

🐘 **PostgreSQL** struggled:  
- **RAM climbed to ~5.5GB**.  
- **CPU was maxed out** on both cores.  

#### **3. Mixed Workload (Reads + Writes)**
- **Redis still won** in req/sec and latency.  
- PostgreSQL remained **CPU-bound**.  

### **Key Takeaways**  
- Redis is **clearly better** for caching—higher throughput, lower latency, and more efficient resource use.  
- PostgreSQL *can* work for caching (especially with optimizations like **unlogged tables**), but it’s **not ideal** for high-performance scenarios.  
- The biggest surprise? The **HTTP server became the bottleneck** before Redis did!  

**Verdict:** If you need **blazing-fast caching, stick with Redis**. But if you’re already using Postgres and caching needs are modest, an unlogged table might suffice.

### Demand for human radiologists is at an all-time high

#### [Submission URL](https://www.worksinprogress.news/p/why-ai-isnt-replacing-radiologists) | 416 points | by [bensouthwood](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=bensouthwood) | [418 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45372335)

### **AI Meets Reality: Why Radiologists Are Still in High Demand**  

Tech experts once warned that AI would make radiologists obsolete—especially since radiology involves clear benchmarks, digital images, and pattern recognition. Yet, despite AI models outperforming humans in detecting diseases like pneumonia and lung cancer, the demand for human radiologists is **higher than ever**, with salaries soaring to an average of **$520,000** in 2025.  

### **Why AI Hasn’t Replaced Radiologists (Yet)**  

1. **Benchmarks ≠ Real-World Success**  
   - AI models excel in controlled tests but struggle with real-world variations—scans from different hospitals, uncommon conditions, or rare imaging quirks.  

2. **Legal & Regulatory Barriers**  
   - Despite AI detecting diseases faster, regulators and insurers hesitate to approve **fully autonomous** diagnostic tools. Human oversight is still required.  

3. **Radiologists Do More Than Just Diagnose**  
   - A radiologist’s job includes consulting with doctors, explaining results to patients, and clinical decision-making—tasks AI can’t yet replicate.  

### **The Limits of Today’s AI in Radiology**  
- Most AI models focus on **a single condition** (like lung nodules) rather than providing a full analysis.  
- About **60% of FDA-approved radiology AI tools** focus on just three areas: stroke, lung cancer, and breast cancer—leaving many subspecialties uncovered.  
- **"Islands of automation"** mean hospitals must juggle multiple AI systems, each addressing a tiny slice of diagnostics.  

### **The Takeaway**  
AI is revolutionizing radiology, but **not replacing radiologists** anytime soon. For now, AI serves as a powerful assistant—enhancing speed and accuracy but still needing human judgment, legal frameworks, and broader capabilities to take over entirely.
