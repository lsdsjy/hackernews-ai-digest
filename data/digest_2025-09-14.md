- **How it works:** Grapevine canes are dried, ground, and processed to extract cellulose, which is then cast into plastic-like films.

### **Why This Matters**  
- **Reduces agricultural waste:** Instead of burning or composting pruned vines, they can be repurposed.  
- **Health & environmental benefits:** Eliminates microplastic pollution, offering a cleaner alternative.  

### **The Future of Packaging?**

#### **From Nicotine Delivery to Web Hosting**  
Who knew your discarded vape could moonlight as a web server? Tinkerer and hacker Bogdan Ionescu turned a disposable vape into a functioning (if slightly sluggish) website host, showcasing the surprising capabilities of hardware typically destined for the trash.  

#### **The Tiny Tech Inside Your Vape**  
Modern disposable vapes often pack more than just flavored vapor—some include **ARM Cortex-M0+ microcontrollers**, USB-C charging, and even labeled debug pins. Ionescu noticed a **PUYA-branded flash microcontroller** inside several vapes and reverse-engineered it into a makeshift server with:

- **24KB of flash storage** (just enough for a tiny website)  
- **3KB of RAM** (yes, *kilobytes*)  

#### **Dial-Up Internet, but on a Vape**  
To get this microscopic server online, Ionescu used:  
1. **Semihosting** – A debugging trick to redirect I/O between the microcontroller and a host PC.  
2. **SLIP (Serial Line Internet Protocol)** – A relic of dial-up internet repurposed to send IP packets over the vape’s serial connection.  
3. **uIP** – A featherweight TCP/IP stack designed for embedded systems.  

The first attempt was painfully slow (**20+ second page loads**), but after optimizing with a ring buffer for batching data, performance improved significantly.  

#### **The Verdict: It Works… Technically**  
While you won’t be hosting Netflix on an ex-vape anytime soon, this project proves how even "disposable" electronics can be repurposed into functional (if janky) hardware.

### OCSP Service Has Reached End of Life

#### [Submission URL](https://letsencrypt.org/2025/08/06/ocsp-service-has-reached-end-of-life) | 207 points | by [pfexec](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=pfexec) | [76 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45242591)

**Let’s Encrypt Kills OCSP to Protect Privacy—Here’s What It Means**  

*Big changes in SSL/TLS: Let’s Encrypt just shut down its OCSP service, ending a privacy-risk era.*  

### **Why OCSP Had to Go**  
In a move announced last December, Let’s Encrypt officially discontinued its **Online Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP) service** today, August 6, 2025. The decision wasn’t made lightly—it was driven by **privacy concerns and operational efficiency**.  

- **Privacy Risks:** OCSP leaks sensitive data—when you visit a site, your browser checks certificate status via OCSP, revealing **your IP and the site you’re visiting** to the certificate authority (CA). Even if a CA (like Let’s Encrypt) doesn’t log this data, leaks or legal demands could expose user activity.  
- **Simpler Infrastructure:** CRLs (Certificate Revocation Lists) offer revocation checks **without real-time tracking**, reducing complexity and operational costs.  

### **The Big Shift to CRLs**  
From now on, revocation checks **will rely solely on CRLs**, a more privacy-friendly method where browsers periodically download a list of revoked certificates rather than querying per-visit.  

### **The Numbers Behind the Shutdown**  
- At its peak, Let’s Encrypt’s OCSP handled **340 billion requests per month**—**140,000 per second** at the CDN layer and 15,000/sec at origin.  
- **Akamai** provided free CDN support for a decade—major kudos for helping sustain the service.  

**Final Take:** Let’s Encrypt’s move marks the **end of an era**—one where speed and convenience came at a privacy cost. While CRLs aren’t perfect, they offer a cleaner, more sustainable future for certificate validation.

# **The Spiral Effect: How Small Moments Shape Our Work Lives**  

### **A Bad Day at Work vs. a Career-Defining Spiral**  

We've all had that moment—late to a meeting, flustered, scrambling to join audio, only to be met with a comment that stings a little. For a **senior team member**, it’s water off a duck’s back: a chuckle, a quick excuse, and they move on. But for a **junior employee**, that same moment can spiral into self-doubt, misinterpretations, and even long-term career consequences.  

### **The Anatomy of a Negative Spiral**  
1. **A Core Question Goes Unanswered** – "Do I belong here?" "Am I good enough?" These questions lurk in the background of our minds, especially when we're new or uncertain.  
2. **A Minor Incident Triggers Doubt** – Your boss’s slightly sarcastic "Glad you could fit us in" hits differently when you’re already questioning your place.  
3. **Ambiguity Gets Framed as Evidence** – Every glance, joke, or silence becomes confirmation: *They don’t respect me. I don’t fit in.*  
4. **Self-Defeating Actions Follow** – Maybe you withdraw, perform poorly, or even lash out—creating a **feedback loop** that reinforces the narrative.  
5. **The Story Solidifies** – Fast-forward months later, and you've either left the job or are trapped in a cycle of strained relationships.  

### **The Power of Construal: Seeing What We Fear**  
Our brains don't just *observe* reality—they **construct** it. Like the students who heard "Tripoli" or "Triple E" based on priming, we interpret events through the lens of our biggest **unanswered questions**.  
- If you're worried about belonging, **every inside joke feels exclusionary**.  
- If you're questioning your competence, **every minor critique feels like proof of failure**.  

### **Breaking the Spiral (or Starting a Positive One)**  
The good news? **Spirals can be reversed—or even launched upward.** Some key strategies:  
✔ **Name Your Core Question** – Simply acknowledging, *"I’m worried I don’t belong"* can help detach from the emotion.  
✔ **Reframe the Evidence** – Was your boss really snarky, or just making a joke they’d use on anyone?  
✔ **Choose a Different Response** – Instead of withdrawing, lean in. A lighthearted, self-aware reply can reset the dynamic.  
✔ **Build Reinforcing Positive Moments** – Small wins, shared laughter, and deliberate acts of belonging can shift the script entirely.  

**Bottom Line:** The difference between a fleeting bad moment and a destructive spiral isn’t just **what happens**—it’s **what we do with it**. The same mechanisms that doom us can also empower us—if we learn to steer them.

# Crafting a Minimal RISC-V Kernel in Zig: A Modern OS Journey  

## A Modern Twist on Classic OS Development  

A developer shares their journey creating a barebones RISC-V kernel in Zig—a fresh take on operating system fundamentals that many computer science students typically explore in C. This modern implementation focuses on core concepts like time-sharing, system calls, and memory virtualization while leveraging RISC-V's simplicity and Zig's clean toolchain.  

## Key Features of the Experiment  

- **Time-sharing kernel** that switches between user threads at set intervals  
- **Unikernel design** where kernel and application compile to a single binary  
- **RISC-V privilege levels** (M-mode → S-mode → U-mode) used effectively  
- **Modern tooling** via Zig for easier cross-compilation to RISC-V  
- **Interactive elements** like timer interrupts and basic I/O via OpenSBI  

## Why This Approach Stands Out  

Unlike traditional cooperative scheduling (where threads explicitly yield control), this kernel implements **preemptive multitasking**—the timer forcibly switches threads, making the system feel like each thread has dedicated CPU time. The author chose RISC-V for its clean architecture and Zig for its simplicity, avoiding the often messy cross-compilation headaches of C.  

### Under the Hood: How the Kernel Works  
- **Thread virtualization**: Each thread gets its own stack and register state  
- **Interrupt handling**: The timer fires periodically, triggering context switches  
- **User/kernel separation**: System calls allow user threads to request kernel services  

> *"RISC-V is an amazing technology that is easy to understand more quickly than other CPU architectures, while remaining a popular choice for many new systems."*

The full code is [available on GitHub](https://github.com/popovicu94/riscv-zig-kernel), and the post includes recommended readings for those new to RISC-V or OS development. Whether you're a student or a curious engineer, this project offers a hands-on way to explore the magic behind how operating systems orchestrate multiple tasks seamlessly.

# **AMD's Mysterious PSP Firmware: The Hidden Hurdles of Coreboot on a Turin System**

### **The Challenge**
Getting Coreboot to run on the Gigabyte MZ33-AR1 with AMD's Turin CPU—one of the newest server processors—wasn't as straightforward as hoped. Even with AMD releasing critical firmware blobs required for initialization, the **Platform Security Processor (PSP)** refused to release the CPU from reset. The only workaround was injecting Coreboot into the vendor firmware—a clunky solution that demanded deeper investigation.

### **The PSP Firmware Puzzle**
Modern x86 CPUs don’t boot alone—they rely on **co-processors like AMD’s PSP (aka ASP)** running specialized firmware before the main CPU even starts. Unlike Intel’s ME, where firmware is neatly separated in flash, AMD’s PSP **blobs are packed inside nested directory structures** (EFS, PSP, BIOS), making debugging a nightmare.

### **Breaking Down the Blobs**
Using the `amdfwtool` (recently improved for better debugging), researchers analyzed vendor and Coreboot images side-by-side. Key findings:
- **SPI speeds** were misconfigured in Coreboot (66MHz vs vendor’s 33MHz), risking instability on the SPI bus.
- **eSPI settings** were off—critical for BMC (management controller) communication.
- A mysterious **"Multi Gen EFS" value** differed between images—still not fully understood.

### **The Fixes**
- **SPI speeds were adjusted** to **33MHz** to match vendor firmware.
- **eSPI configs were corrected**, ensuring BMC debug features like serial output would work.
- Coreboot’s `amdfwtool` gained extra parsing power, helping demystify the blob hierarchy.

### **The Unresolved Mystery**
Despite fixes, the **Multi Gen EFS discrepancy** remains unexplained. Could this be why the PSP holds the CPU in reset? **The hunt for answers continues.**

### **What's Next?**
With SPI and eSPI corrected, the path to seamless **Coreboot support** looks clearer—but **AMD’s PSP black box still hides secrets**. Future work will tackle:
1. Full reverse-engineering of PSP firmware logic.
2. Pinpointing the role of **Multi Gen EFS**.
3. **Eliminating vendor firmware dependence** for true open-source booting.
