### A brief history of threads and threading

#### [Submission URL](https://eclecticlight.co/2025/09/20/a-brief-history-of-threads-and-threading/) | 81 points | by [emschwartz](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=emschwartz) | [22 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45317526)

**From 8 MHz to Multicore Mayhem: The Evolution of Mac Multitasking**  

In 1984, the original Macintosh boasted a single 8 MHz Motorola 68000 CPU—so primitive by today’s standards that it could only run one app at a time. Fast forward to today, where Macs juggle dozens of apps, background tasks, and system processes effortlessly. How did we get here?  

### **The Early Days: One App at a Time**  
Back in 1985, Apple engineer Andy Hertzfeld revolutionized computing by writing **Switcher**, a utility that allowed users to—wait for it—*switch* between multiple apps (though still only one ran at any given moment). It was a baby step, but a crucial one.  

By 1987, **MultiFinder** replaced Switcher, introducing **cooperative multitasking**—where apps had to "play nice" and voluntarily yield processing time to others. The catch? A single rogue app could freeze the whole system by refusing to share.  

### **Enter Preemptive Multitasking**  
In 1988, Apple dabbled with Unix (A/UX), which brought **preemptive multitasking**—where the OS forcibly manages tasks, preventing any single app from hogging resources. By System 7.5.3 (1996), this feature made its way into classic Mac OS, though app compatibility remained a headache.  

### **The PPC & Intel Era: Multiple Cores Arrive**  
- **2000**: Power Mac G4 introduced **dual-processor support**, a game-changer for power users.  
- **2005**: The Power Mac G5 brought **dual-core** CPUs, doubling processing muscle.  
- **2006**: Intel’s **Core Duo** chips hit Apple’s iMac, cementing the multicore future.  

### **Mac OS X & Grand Central Dispatch (GCD)**  
When Mac OS X launched, it embraced **preemptive multitasking** and threads (Mach, POSIX, Cocoa)—though managing them was complex. Then came **Grand Central Dispatch (GCD) in Snow Leopard (2009)**, a smarter way to manage tasks.  

GCD functions like a **virtual traffic controller**, intelligently balancing workloads across CPU cores. No longer did developers have to micromanage threads—instead, they could just submit tasks, and GCD handled the rest.  

### **The Apple Silicon Revolution**  
Today’s M-series chips take parallelism to the extreme, with **high-performance and efficiency cores** intelligently handling different workloads. Background tasks? Time Machine backups? Video rendering? No problem—multitasking is now **effortless**.  

### **Conclusion**  
From a single 8 MHz chip struggling to run a word processor to today’s M-series Macs juggling AI, multitasking, and high-performance apps—**the journey has been astonishing**. And with **Dispatch (GCD), Swift concurrency, and hardware acceleration**, Apple’s still pushing the limits.  

What’s next? Probably more cores, smarter scheduling, and maybe even apps that manage themselves. One thing’s for sure: **the future of multitasking is even wilder.** 🚀
