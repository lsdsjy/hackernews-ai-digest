### Litestream v0.5.0

#### [Submission URL](https://fly.io/blog/litestream-v050-is-here/) | 197 points | by [emschwartz](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=emschwartz) | [81 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45453936)

# **Litestream Gets a Major Upgrade: Faster Backups & Point-in-Time Recovery for SQLite**  

Ben Johnson, the creator of **Litestream**, has unveiled a significant update to the popular SQLite backup and restore tool. If you’re building full-stack apps with SQLite, this is big news—Litestream now offers **faster performance** and **efficient point-in-time recovery (PITR)**.  

### **What’s Litestream?**  
Litestream acts as a **"silent guardian"** for SQLite databases, running in the background as a sidecar process. It **intercepts WAL (Write-Ahead Log) checkpoints** and streams them to object storage (like S3) in real time. If your server crashes, **you can restore your SQLite database quickly**—with minimal downtime.  

### **From Litestream to LiteFS and Back**  
Johnson originally built **LiteFS**, a more advanced SQLite replication system designed for **multi-region setups** (like Postgres read-replicas). However, the market **gravitated toward Litestream** for its simplicity, so he’s now bringing the best of **LiteFS’s tech back into Litestream**.  

### **The LTX File Format: The Secret Sauce**  
The biggest upgrade involves **LTX**, a transaction-aware file format originally created for LiteFS. Unlike raw page-level backups, LTX **compacts multiple changes into efficient, deduplicated files**, making **database restoration much faster**.  

- **Level 1:** Compacts changes within a **30-second window**  
- **Level 2:** Compacts Level 1 files into **5-minute chunks**  
- **Level 3:** Further compacts Level 2 files into **hourly snapshots**  

This means **restoring a database** now requires fetching **far fewer files**, drastically cutting recovery time.  

### **Goodbye, Generations (No More Multiverse Madness)**  
Old Litestream used "generations" (like MCU timelines) to manage sync gaps between multiple backups. Now, **LTX eliminates generations entirely**, simplifying the process while improving reliability.  

### **Why This Matters for Your SQLite Apps**  
- **Resilience:** No more sweating over server failures—your DB can be restored in minutes.  
- **Speed:** LTX enables **faster restores** with fewer I/O operations.  
- **Simplicity:** Still the same easy-to-deploy tool developers love.  

Litestream continues to be **open source**, runs anywhere, and remains a **perfect fit** for apps that want SQLite’s simplicity **without sacrificing durability**.

# The Rise and Fall of Yogh: The Strangest Letter in the English Alphabet  

English spelling is famously chaotic—silent letters, inconsistent rules, and a mishmash of influences from Latin, French, and Germanic roots. But long before "i before e" became a headache, there was **yogh (Ȝ/ȝ)**, an obscure medieval letter that once played a vital role in written English. Linguist Colin Gorrie dives into the strange tale of this forgotten character and how its disappearance made English spelling even more confusing.  

## The Origins of Yogh: From Insular 'G' to a Letter of Its Own  

- In Old English, scribes used the **insular 'ᵹ'** (resembling a mix of 'z' and '3') to represent the sound we now write as 'g.'  
- After the Norman Conquest (1066), French-trained scribes brought the Carolingian 'g,' relegating the insular form to the shadows.  
- By the 12th century, English scribes revived a version of the insular 'ᵹ'—now called **yogh (ȝ)**—but gave it a new role.  

## A Letter with Two Lives  

Yogh wasn’t just a quirky 'g'—it served two distinct purposes:  
1. **The 'y' sound** (e.g., *ȝong* → *young*).  
2. A **guttural "ch" sound** (like the 'ch' in Scottish *loch* or German *Bach*), which no longer exists in modern English.  

Fun fact: The word *yogh* itself was originally written as *ȝoȝ*!  

## Why Did Yogh Disappear?  

- **Printing press woes**: Early printers lacked a yogh type, often replacing it with 'z' or 'gh'—leading to bizarre spellings like *McKenȝie* → *McKenzie*.  
- **French influence**: Norman scribes preferred 'g' and 'y,' pushing yogh into obsolescence.  
- **Sound shifts**: The guttural ȝ sound faded from English, making the letter redundant.  

## The Ghost of Yogh Lingers  

Though yogh vanished, its legacy lives on:  
- The 'z' in *Menzies* (originally *Mengȝis*).  
- The silent 'gh' in *night* and *thought* (once pronounced with that lost guttural sound).  

**Bottom Line**: Yogh’s demise left a gap filled by inconsistent spelling, contributing to English’s notorious complexity.

### **From Couch to 10,000 Pushups: How a Simple Goal Transformed My Life**  

**The Wakeup Call**  
As a CTO and serial SaaS builder (SecurityBot.dev, 6DollarCRM), I spent years glued to a laptop—my fitness was nonexistent, and my kids outpaced me in energy. Watching my parents face health struggles was the final nudge: *I had to change*.  

**The (Harebrained) Idea**  
On January 1, 2025, I committed to **10,000 pushups in a year**. No gym, no gear—just me, the floor, and a Google Sheet tracking every rep.  

### **The Journey**  

**January: The Honeymoon Phase**  
- ✅ **1,018 pushups** (32.8/day)  
- 🏃 **One treadmill run** (2.17 miles)  
- 🍔 **Quit fast food forever** after a nauseating McDonald’s incident  

**February: Winter Slump**  
- 📉 Only **848 pushups** (but strength improved—hit a daily high of **117**)  
- 🏋️♂️ Dabbles with basement weights (mostly while listening to CNBC)  

**March: The Struggle Was Real**  
- ❄️ **Just 9 days** of pushups (206 total)  
- Blame it on seasonal depression (or laziness)  

**April: The Comeback**  
- ☀️ **375 pushups** + **6 weight sessions**  
- 🍷 **Quit wine** (hangovers were ruining my sleep—now it’s Miller Lite or nothing)  

### **The Unexpected Wins**  
- **No more fast food** = feeling *way* better  
- **Alcohol cutback** = better sleep, fewer regrets  
- **Pushups = habit** (even on lazy days)  

Could a simple challenge like this work for you? **Spoiler:** By September, I was a new person—healthier, stronger, and proving that small steps lead to big changes.
