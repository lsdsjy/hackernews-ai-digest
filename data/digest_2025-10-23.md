### **Why the Universe Exists: A 19th-Century Knot Theory Might Hold the Key**  

**From discarded atomic theory to cosmic origins** – In 1867, physicist Lord Kelvin envisioned atoms as knots in the aether. Though proven wrong, this idea might finally have found its purpose—explaining *why the universe exists at all.*  

New research from Japanese physicists unveils a radical possibility: **knots in quantum fields** could have briefly dominated the early universe, tilting the cosmic scales in favor of matter over antimatter—and leaving behind echoes we might soon detect.  

#### **The Problem: Where Did All the Antimatter Go?**  
- The Big Bang should’ve created **equal parts matter and antimatter**, annihilating into pure energy.  
- Yet, the observable universe is almost entirely made of **matter**. For every billion collisions, *one lone particle survived*.  
- The **Standard Model of physics can’t explain this imbalance**, leaving a glaring mystery.  

#### **The Solution: Cosmic Knots & Symmetry Breaking**  
Hiroshima University researchers **Muneto Nitta, Minoru Eto, and Yu Hamada** merged two key extensions of known physics:  
1. **Peccei-Quinn (PQ) Symmetry** – Solves a major neutron mystery and introduces the *axion*, a leading dark matter candidate.  
2. **B-L Symmetry** – Explains why *neutrinos have mass* and brings in *right-handed neutrinos* (key to baryogenesis).  

When these symmetries broke in the early universe, they could have spawned **knot-like solitons**—entangled quantum fields that stored energy in a metastable state. These **"cosmic knots"** may have briefly dominated the universe, collapsing just enough to **favor matter over antimatter.**  

#### **Gravitational Waves Could Reveal the Truth**  
If these knots existed, their death throes might have left **a faint hum in spacetime**—a gravitational wave signal we could detect in the future.  

**Why it matters:**  
- Explains *why anything exists* instead of total annihilation.  
- Offers testable predictions, a rarity in cosmology.  
- Bridges **particle physics** (neutrinos, axions) and **cosmology** (dark matter, baryogenesis).  

*Original research in [Physical Review Letters](https://journals.aps.org/prl/).*
