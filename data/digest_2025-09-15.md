**"Turning a Disposable Vape Into a Blazingly Slow Web Server"**  

### **The Unlikely Web Host**  
Imagine hosting a website... on a disposable vape. That’s exactly what Bogdan Ionescu set out to do, pushing a tiny ARM Cortex-M0+ microcontroller to its limits. The result? A functional (if not particularly speedy) web server running on hardware so cheap it’s literally meant to be thrown away.  

### **From Vape to Server**  
Disposable vapes have evolved—many now come with USB-C charging and even debug pins (thanks, big tobacco engineers!). After collecting discarded vapes, Bogdan discovered they contained **PY32 microcontrollers**, which, despite their modest specs (**24MHz CPU, 24KB flash, 3KB RAM**), proved surprisingly capable.  

### **The Janky Modem Trick**  
No Wi-Fi? No problem! By leveraging **SLIP (Serial Line Internet Protocol)**, Bogdan turned a serial connection into a makeshift "modem" using **semihosting** (debugging syscalls for ARM chips). With some Python, `socat`, and a bit of Linux networking magic, he managed to route IP traffic through the vape’s debug interface.  

### **A Web Server on 3KB of RAM**  
The **uIP TCP/IP stack** provided just enough networking chops to serve HTTP requests, though initial performance was hilariously bad:  
- **1.5-second ping times**  
- **50% packet loss**  
- **20+ seconds to load a basic page**  

But with a few optimizations (buffering reads/writes), speed improved—though "blazingly fast" remains a relative term.  

### **Why? Because We Can.**  
This is peak hacker spirit: taking garbage (literally) and making it do something ridiculously impractical just for the fun of it. If you want to check out the project, **[the demo is live](http://example.com)**—just don’t expect Netflix.

### Why do we keep gravitating toward complexity?

#### [Submission URL](https://kyrylo.org/software/2025/08/21/why-do-software-developers-love-complexity.html) | 136 points | by [PaulHoule](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=PaulHoule) | [163 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45256043)

### **From Pyramids to Penzillas: Why We Love Complexity Even When Simplicity Works**  

**TL;DR:** We worship complexity because marketing dazzles us, our brains mistake it for intelligence, and we love a good engineering challenge—even when simpler solutions work just fine.  

---  

#### **The Monuments of Modern Tech**  
The Great Pyramids took decades, thousands of workers, and immense planning. Today’s digital "pyramids"—our bloated software stacks—also take years, endless frameworks, and layers of dependencies. But unlike the pyramids (which are still standing), our codebases often crumble under their own weight.  

Yet instead of stripping things down, we keep adding features. Why? **Because complexity sells.**  

---  

#### **Sell Me This Pen (Zilla Edition) 🚀**  
- **Regular Pen:** It writes. That’s it. Boring.  
- **Penzilla:** A pen that also tweets, syncs to your smart fridge, charges your phone, and doubles as a panic button (for when your code fails in prod).  

Which one would you **market**? Which one would people **buy**?  

Now swap "pen" with **`cat`**. The Unix `cat` command is perfect for what it does—concatenating files. But **`catzilla`**? Now we’re talking monitoring, remoting, AI-powered file insights—**this excites investors**!  

The problem? *Nobody actually needs most of this.* But **social proof + hype = adoption**, even if it’s unnecessary.  

---  

#### **Why Our Brains Love Complexity**  
- **It feels smart.** If it looks complicated, we assume it’s important. (See: corporate buzzwords, over-engineered frameworks.)  
- **It signals effort.** Layers of abstraction convince stakeholders that serious work is happening. (Even if it’s just job security theater.)  
- **It feeds creativity.** Solving intricate problems is **intellectually satisfying**—even if a simpler solution exists.  

But complexity has a dark side: maintenance nightmares, slower performance, and the dreaded **“what idiot wrote this? …Oh, it was me.”** moments.  

---  

#### **React vs. Vanilla JS: A Status Symbol?**  
Want to trigger a room full of devs? Say **“You don’t need React for that.”**  

React adds hooks, state management, SSR, hydration, a build pipeline, and a cult-like following. But often, **plain JS + DOM manipulation would work fine.**  

So why does React win?  
✅ **Marketing** (Facebook-backed! Ecosystem!)  
✅ **Social proof** (Everyone’s using it—you look outdated if you don’t.)  
✅ **The illusion of productivity** (Fancy tools = perceived speed, even if they add overhead.)  

Vanilla JS doesn’t sell itself. It just works. And in the long run? **Simple solutions often outlast the hype trains.**  

---  

#### **Why We Keep Building Cathedrals of Code**  
1. **Creative Ego** – Over-engineering is **fun**. Architects didn’t *need* flying buttresses, but damn, they look cool.  
2. **Legacy Spaghetti** – Once complexity is in, refactoring is a luxury few can afford.  
3. **Team Dynamics** – Everyone adds their own “just in case” abstractions, and suddenly, no one understands the system.  
4. **The Innovation Trap** – If your competitors have 10 features, you need 11—even if users only use two.  

---  

### **Final Lesson: Build Like an Egyptian**  
If you’re going to make a pyramid:  
- **Have a clear purpose.** (Avoid "just because" features.)  
- **Minimize hollow chambers.** (Delete unused code.)  
- **Design for longevity.** (Would you want to debug this in 5 years?)  

Otherwise, you’re not building a monument—**you’re stacking bricks until they collapse.**  

So next time you reach for another dependency or abstraction… ask:  
❓ **Is this solving a real problem, or am I just flexing?**  
❓ **Will users notice—or just future devs cursing my name?**  

Because in the end, **simplicity survives.**

# The Chilling Lifecycle of Pig-Butchering Scams: How Romance Turns Into Financial Ruin  

A groundbreaking new study lifts the veil on one of the most devastating online scams today—pig-butchering schemes that combine romance fraud with sophisticated financial manipulation. Researchers from [arXiv:2503.20821](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2503.20821) conducted in-depth interviews with **26 victims** to map out the ruthless mechanics behind these cons.  

## **From “Hello, is this Anna?” to Financial Ruin**  
Pig-butchering scams (named for "fattening up" victims before the slaughter) follow a disturbingly calculated process:  

1. **The Hook**: Scammers initiate contact through dating apps, social media, or even "wrong-number" texts, often posing as potential romantic partners.  
2. **The Slow Burn**: Perpetrators build trust over weeks or months, sharing fake personal stories before pivoting to financial advice.  
3. **The Investment Trap**: Victims are lured into fake trading platforms where they see fabricated "profits," tricking them into investing more.  
4. **The Squeeze**: After draining victims’ funds, scammers disappear—but often circle back for follow-up cons when victims are most vulnerable.  

Shockingly, **many victims were pressed into taking loans** after exhausting savings, compounding their financial devastation.  

## **Why Victims Stay Trapped**  
The research highlights psychological manipulation techniques, including:  
- **Emotional blackmail**: "If you loved me, you’d invest with me."  
- **FOMO pressure**: Fake testimonials and urgent deadlines push victims to act.  
- **Repetition**: Many victims fell for **secondary scams**—sometimes by the same criminals—after the initial fraud.  

## **Breaking the Cycle**  
The study calls for **interventions** like:  
✅ **Platform detection** of scammer grooming patterns on social media.  
✅ **Bank and payment processors flagging suspicious transfers** tied to known fraud networks.  
✅ **Non-stigmatizing language** (e.g., avoiding "victim-blaming") to encourage reporting.  

## **Final Thought**  
These scams thrive on isolation and shame. By exposing their inner workings, researchers hope to disrupt a **multibillion-dollar criminal industry**—and prevent the next "Anna" from becoming a statistic.

### GuitarPie: Electric Guitar Fretboard Pie Menus

#### [Submission URL](https://andreasfender.com/publications.php) | 46 points | by [DonHopkins](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=DonHopkins) | [5 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45250328)

### **Hacker News Digest: Innovative Human-Computer Interaction Breakthroughs**  

#### **Using an Electric Guitar as a Pie Menu Controller**  
**[GuitarPie](https://example.com)** turns your electric guitar's fretboard into a controller for digital interfaces, eliminating the need to switch between playing and using a mouse/keyboard. By recognizing audio input from an unmodified electric guitar, **GuitarPie** maps UI interactions to fret positions—no need to memorize chords. The team also built **TabCtrl**, a tablature app that leverages this method for seamless playback control. *No extra hardware needed—just play!* 🎸  

#### **Mobile Pen Input Without a Drawing Tablet (OptiBasePen)**  
Tired of bulky drawing tablets? **OptiBasePen** enables high-fidelity digital input on *any* surface using a two-part system:  
- A **base unit** (tracked via mouse sensor) follows hand movements.  
- A **pen** with IR sensors tracks its position relative to the base.  
The result? **No drift, no fixed input area, and natural pen-on-paper feel**—perfect for artists on the move. ✍️  

#### **PressurePick: Detecting Muscle Tension in Guitarists**  
Playing guitar with tense muscles can lead to bad habits. **PressurePick** uses embedded pressure sensors in picks and the guitar neck to detect tension, helping players identify problem areas in their technique. Early tests with 12 guitarists show promising results—could this be the future of smart practice? 🎶  

#### **InfinitePaint: VR Painting with Real Water Brushes & Paper**  
Painting in VR usually lacks tactile feedback. **InfinitePaint** fixes that by using **real wet brushes on water-sensitive paper**, which temporarily turns black when painted on. A camera digitizes strokes while VR overlays digital effects—blending physical and digital art seamlessly. Artists call it more natural than drawing tablets! 🎨  

#### **DeltaPen: A Drift-Free Pen for Any Surface**  
Modern digital pens struggle with **rotation sensing** and **drift** on passive surfaces. **DeltaPen** solves this by using **dual optical flow sensors** to track movement *and* rotation—without external trackers. Add haptic feedback, pressure sensitivity, and tilt support, and you’ve got a **truly portable drafting tool**. ✏️  

### **Why This Matters**  
These innovations blur the lines between physical and digital interaction—whether you're a musician, artist, or designer, HCI research is making creative tools **more intuitive and immersive**.
