# Study Warns of Toxic Metal Buildup from MRI Contrast Agents  

A new study published in *Magnetic Resonance Imaging* has uncovered a concerning reaction between a common MRI contrast agent and dietary compounds, potentially explaining why some patients retain harmful metal nanoparticles long after scans.  

### 🔍 Key Findings:  
- **Gadolinium**, used in MRI contrast dyes, may react with **oxalic acid** (found in foods like spinach and produced when digesting vitamin C), forming toxic nanoparticles.  
- These particles can linger in the brain, kidneys, and blood for years, linked to **nephrogenic systemic fibrosis (NSF)**—a rare but serious condition.  
- Nearly **half of affected patients** had retained gadolinium after just **one** MRI scan, suggesting individual biology plays a role.  
- Lead researcher Dr. Brent Wagner now **avoids vitamin C** before MRIs, citing its potential to trigger gadolinium reactivity.  

### 🧪 What’s Next?  
Researchers are building an **international patient registry** to track gadolinium retention patterns and identify high-risk individuals through blood, urine, and hair samples.  

📌 **Why It Matters:** While gadolinium enhances MRI clarity, this discovery highlights unseen risks and could lead to revised safety protocols—especially for patients with metabolic conditions or high oxalic acid levels.

### Debian Technical Committee overrides systemd change

#### [Submission URL](https://lwn.net/Articles/1041316/) | 204 points | by [birdculture](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=birdculture) | [196 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45692915)

### **Debian's Systemd Clash: Lock Files at the Heart of a Policy Debate**  
**Old standards vs. modern security—can Debian find a balance?**

A recent change in **systemd v258**—where `/run/lock` permissions were tightened—has sparked a heated debate in the Debian community. The issue? Many legacy programs (like **UUCP** and **cu**) still rely on this directory being **world-writable**, a practice that systemd maintainers argue is a **security risk**.  

#### 🔐 **The Conflict: Security vs. Compatibility**  
- **Systemd’s stance**: `/run/lock` was deprecated in v258 and set to **root-only** by default. Systemd maintainers **Lennart Poettering** and **Luca Boccassi** argue that a **world-writable `/run` is dangerous**—malicious users could fill it up, crashing critical services like **udev**.  
- **Debian’s dilemma**: **Debian Policy** still follows the outdated **Filesystem Hierarchy Standard (FHS)**, which mandates **world-writable `/var/lock`** (symbolically linked to `/run/lock`).  

#### ⚖️ **The Technical Committee Steps In**  
Since systemd **marked its bug "WONTFIX,"** Debian's **Technical Committee (TC)** was forced to intervene. They ruled:  
- The systemd package **must comply with Debian Policy**, meaning `/run/lock` must remain **world-writable**—for now.  
- However, they urged maintainers to **migrate software away from lock files** and switch to safer alternatives like `flock()`.  

#### 💡 **Why This Matters**  
- **Debian stable (trixie) remains unaffected** (running systemd v257).  
- **Debian unstable (forky) must comply**, leaving maintainers scrambling to patch affected software.  
- The **FHS is effectively abandoned**, so future Debian policies may evolve—but **legacy software won’t disappear overnight**.  

#### 🔮 **Looking Ahead**  
This conflict highlights the struggle between maintaining **compatibility** and **security** in Linux systems. While systemd pushes **modernization**, Debian must juggle **policy compliance**, **legacy software**, and **user expectations**.  

**What’s the solution?**  
Either:  
1. **Update legacy programs** (like UUCP) to stop using `/run/lock`.  
2. **Patch systemd’s defaults** via `/etc/tmpfiles.d/`—but that’s just a band-aid.  

One thing’s clear: **Debian’s compromise won’t please everyone.**
