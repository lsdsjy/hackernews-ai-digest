# **SedonaDB: A Single-Node Geospatial Powerhouse Arrives**  

The **Apache Sedona** team has unveiled **SedonaDB**—a **lightning-fast, single-node analytical database built for geospatial data**.  

### **Why SedonaDB? 🚀**  
While distributed systems like Spark and Flink handle large-scale geospatial data (via **Apache Sedona**), not every project needs a cluster. **SedonaDB** fills the gap for **small-to-medium workloads**, offering:  
- **Native geospatial support** (no plugins needed!)  
- **Blazing-fast Rust backend**  
- **Python, SQL, R, and Rust APIs**  
- **Seamless integration** with popular formats (GeoParquet, GeoArrow, GeoPandas)  

### **Quick Spatial Join Example 🌍**  
Want to find out which country a city is in? SedonaDB makes **spatial joins** effortless:  
```python  
import sedona.db  
sd = sedona.db.connect()  

# Find cities within countries  
result = sd.sql("""  
    SELECT cities.name as city_name, countries.name as country_name  
    FROM cities  
    JOIN countries ON ST_Intersects(cities.geometry, countries.geometry)  
""").show()  
```  
**Output**:  
```
┌───────────────┬─────────────────────────────┐  
│ city_name     │ country_name               │  
├───────────────┼─────────────────────────────┤  
│ Vatican City  │ Italy                      │  
│ San Marino    │ San Marino                 │  
└───────────────┴─────────────────────────────┘  
```  

### **Performance Benchmarked 📊**  
In **Apache Sedona SpatialBench**, SedonaDB **outperforms** **GeoPandas** and **DuckDB Spatial** in **scalability & balanced query execution**.  

### **Try It Today!**  
Just install via pip:  
```bash  
pip install "apache-sedona[db]"  
```  
🔥 **Perfect for geospatial tasks in local/cloud environments**—no heavy infrastructure required.  

**What’s next?** Future updates will bring **raster data support** and expanded spatial functions.  

[**Learn more on Apache Sedona’s official site.**](https://sedona.apache.org/)

#### [Submission URL](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cevz13xkxpro) | 676 points | by [_zie](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=_zie) | [115 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45358940)

### **Breakthrough in Huntington’s Disease Treatment: Slowing Progression by 75% in World-First Trial**  

For the first time, scientists have successfully treated **Huntington’s disease**, a devastating genetic condition that destroys brain cells, causing symptoms similar to dementia, Parkinson’s, and motor neurone disease. The breakthrough therapy—a cutting-edge **gene-silencing treatment**—has been shown to **slow disease progression by an astonishing 75%**, giving patients potentially **decades of better quality life**.  

#### **A Glimpse of Hope for Patients**  
Huntington’s is a cruel disease—it **runs in families**, with a **50% chance** of inheritance, and symptoms usually appear in a person’s **30s or 40s**, leading to gradual physical and cognitive decline. Until now, there was no cure, only management of symptoms.  

But in a **landmark clinical trial**, researchers observed patients who **stopped deteriorating as expected**. One patient who had retired due to the disease **returned to work**, while others **continued walking despite predictions they’d need a wheelchair**.  

#### **How the Treatment Works**  
The therapy involves **delivering a modified gene therapy** directly into the brain via **a 12- to 18-hour neurosurgical procedure**. Doctors use **MRI-guided microcatheters** to inject a harmless virus carrying a **custom DNA sequence** into two critical brain regions.  

Once inside, the treatment **instructs neurons to produce a molecule that blocks the faulty huntingtin protein**—effectively **preventing brain cells from dying**. Unlike most treatments that require regular doses, this **one-time intervention** is expected to last a lifetime.  

#### **"Breathtaking" Results**  
Lead researcher **Prof. Sarah Tabrizi** (University College London) called the **75% slowing of progression "spectacular"**—far beyond what the team imagined. **Prof. Ed Wild** added that the findings brought him "a bit teary," knowing the profound difference it could make for families.  

However, challenges remain:  
✔ **Accessibility** – The treatment is expected to be **expensive**.  
✔ **Full data review** – Results were announced by biotech firm *uniQure* but have not yet been peer-reviewed.  
✔ **Earlier treatment** – Future studies will explore whether intervening **before symptoms appear** could halt the disease entirely.  

#### **A Brighter Future for Families Like Jack’s**  
**Jack May-Davis, 30**—who inherited the gene that killed his father—told the BBC the breakthrough left him **"overwhelmed" and hopeful for a longer, healthier life.**  

With **licensing applications expected by 2026**, this marks a **new era in treating genetic brain diseases**, offering real hope where there was once only despair.

### How fast is Go? Simulating particles on a smart TV

#### [Submission URL](https://dgerrells.com/blog/how-fast-is-go-simulating-millions-of-particles-on-a-smart-tv) | 95 points | by [lossolo](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=lossolo) | [28 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45364450)

# **"A Million Particles on a Smart TV? Go for It!" – How Fast Is Go, Really?**  

## **The Challenge: Millions of Particles, Zero Client-Side Simulation**  
Ever wondered if Go could simulate *millions of particles* while keeping it multiplayer-compatible and CPU-only? No fancy SIMD, no cheating—just raw goroutine power. After struggling with a real-world WebSocket server problem (despite AI's best efforts), our intrepid coder decided the best way to *really* learn was to build something absurd: a **real-time particle simulation in Go**, benchmarking performance while ensuring it could run on something as constrained as a **Smart TV browser**.

### **Why Determinism and Server-Side Madness?**  
Most multiplayer games use **deterministic simulation**—they sync only player inputs, letting clients compute game states locally. But what if the simulation is too heavy for weak clients? (Think: millions of particles.)  

The solution? **Defer rendering to the server**, just like *Doom 3’s deferred shading* decoupled lighting from polygon count. Instead of sending particle data, the server sends **compressed video frames**, meaning clients just display a stream, no matter how heavy the actual simulation is.  

### **The Math: Data vs. Pixels**  
- **Particles approach**: At 1 million particles (x, y, dx, dy as 4-byte floats), that’s **8-16MB per frame** (bad!).  
- **Framebuffer approach**: 1920x1080 pixels → ~2MB raw (120MB/s at 60fps, but compressed H.264 would drop it to ~260KB/frame).  

→ **4M particles? Framebuffer wins.**  

### **The Results**  
After optimizing Go’s performance (spoiler: it’s *fast enough*), the final version runs **4M particles** and streams to clients smoothly.  

🔗 **Try it live**: [howfastisgo.dev](https://howfastisgo.dev)  
🔗 **GitHub**: [github.com/author/cool-go-particles](https://github.com/author/cool-go-particles)  

### **Conclusion**  
- **Go can handle millions of particles** (even without SIMD).  
- **Cool tricks from game dev** (like deferred rendering) apply to networked simulations.  
- **Sometimes, brute-forcing on the server** is the best solution.  

**TL;DR:** If you ever doubted Go’s speed—try simulating 4M particles while streaming to a Smart TV. *Then* judge. 🚀
