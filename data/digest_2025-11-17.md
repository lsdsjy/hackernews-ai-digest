### **Microsoft Azure Slammed by Massive 15 Tbps DDoS Attack from Aisuru Botnet**  

#### **Record-Breaking Cyber Assault Involves 500,000 Hacked Devices**  

Microsoft’s Azure cloud infrastructure was recently hit by a staggering **15.72 terabits per second (Tbps)** Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attack—one of the largest ever recorded. The attack, launched by the **Aisuru botnet**, originated from **over 500,000 IP addresses**, predominantly compromised IoT devices like routers and cameras in the U.S. and beyond.  

🔹 **Attack Details:**  
- Targeted a single Azure public IP in Australia  
- Peaked at **3.64 billion packets per second (bpps)**  
- Used UDP flood technique with minimal source spoofing  
- Attack lasted **just two minutes** but packed a devastating punch  

#### **Aisuru: The Turbo Mirai-Class Botnet Crushing the Internet**  
Aisuru is a next-gen **Internet of Things (IoT) botnet**, infamous for breaking DDoS records. Earlier in **September 2025**, Cloudflare thwarted an even larger **22.2 Tbps** Aisuru-linked assault—**one of the biggest in history**.  

🚨 **How Did Aisuru Get So Powerful?**  
- Initially exploited **TotoLink router firmware** (infecting 100,000+ devices in one go)  
- Compromises cameras, DVRs, and routers from **T-Mobile, Zyxel, D-Link, and Linksys**  
- Uses a **flood of DNS queries** to boost malicious domains (even overtaking Amazon & Google in Cloudflare’s rankings)  

#### **Cloudflare & Microsoft Fight Back**  
Cloudflare has started **hiding suspected malicious domains** from its "Top Domains" list after discovering Aisuru’s manipulation tactics. Meanwhile, Microsoft continues to improve defenses, but **experts warn these attacks won’t stop soon**.  

💡 **Why This Matters:**  
- IoT devices remain **wildly vulnerable**  
- Attack power is **growing exponentially**  
- **No industry is safe**—corporate clouds, ISPs, and DNS providers all in the crosshairs  

**Stay tuned for updates—this botnet war is far from over.** 🚀

#### [Submission URL](https://www.windowslatest.com/2025/11/18/windows-11-to-add-an-ai-agent-that-runs-in-background-with-access-to-personal-folders-warns-of-security-risk/) | 600 points | by [jinxmeta](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=jinxmeta) | [543 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45959795)

# **Windows 11’s Bold AI Leap: Introducing "Agent Workspace" Amid Backlash**

Microsoft is doubling down on its vision to transform Windows 11 into an "AI-native" operating system, despite growing skepticism from power users. The latest move? **Agent Workspace**, an experimental feature in Insider builds that grants AI agents their own virtual desktop, user account, and **background runtime access** to your files.  

### **What Is Agent Workspace?**  
- **AI with a Desktop** – Agent Workspace gives AI assistants (like future Copilot agents) their own containerized environment—similar to Windows Sandbox but with controlled access to your **Documents, Desktop, Pictures, and Videos folders**.
- **Works Like a Human** – AI agents can simulate user actions: opening apps, typing, navigating files, and even performing tasks like booking flights or managing files—**all in the background**.
- **Optional (For Now)*** – Currently only available in **Dev/Beta Channels**, the feature requires manual activation via Settings > System > AI Components.

### **Why It’s Raising Eyebrows**  
- **Privacy Concerns** – AI agents gain **read/write access** to personal folders by default (though Microsoft insists this is scoped and auditable).
- **Security Risks** – Unlike **Windows Sandbox** (which is fully isolated and wiped after use), Agent Workspace persists and has limited access to your data.  
- **Silicon Valley’s AI Push** – Despite OpenAI and others marketing agents as revolutionary, critics argue they often **underdeliver** on promises.  

### **Microsoft’s Defense**  
The company claims:  
- Agents operate with **runtime isolation** and user-controlled permissions.  
- Activities are logged for transparency.  
- It’s still early—feedback from Insiders will shape its future.  

### **What’s Next?**  
With **Windows 11 24H2** and **25H2** looming, AI integration is *inevitable*. Will Microsoft win over skeptics—or ignite more backlash? Only time (and testing) will tell.
